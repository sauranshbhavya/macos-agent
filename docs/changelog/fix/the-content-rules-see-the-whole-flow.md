Architectural decisions / pitfalls discovered (required, write "none" if true):
- **The money rule had no purchase vocabulary at all, and that is the whole of SONNY-506's checkable half.** Its three lists were built for money *movement* — pay, transfer, payout, refund, payee — so buying was invisible: `git grep -cE '"(buy|purchase|checkout|postage)"' 981c6e56 -- Sources/MacAgentCore/SkillPackContentRules.swift` exits 1 with no output, and 15 of the 17 rows of the new table loaded at that head. The class was reachable by the mechanism already there; nobody had written the words down.
- **The cost of adding them over the shipped corpus is nothing, and the number is re-derivable rather than quoted.** The rule reads one unit per flow, one per summary and one per section, so the population is `python3 -c "import json,glob; p=[json.load(open(x)) for x in glob.glob('Sources/MacAgent/Resources/SkillPacks/*.skillpack.json')]; print(len(p)+sum(len(d['flows'])+len(d['sections']) for d in p))"` → **868** at `c36c8107`: 473 summaries, 395 flows, and 0 sections, because no shipped pack declares any. Every one of those units loads, which is what the whole-corpus test asserts. **The four words that would have cost something were measured out of the lists rather than reasoned out**: bare `checkout` refuses samcart's and thrivecart's summaries, which describe products that *are* checkouts; bare `upgrade` refuses google_meet's step naming the "Teaching and Learning Upgrade" edition; `paid plan` refuses slack's, miro's and manychat's plan preconditions; and `seat` as a money object would refuse an events pack's seating chart. All four are rows of `purchaseWordsThatAreNotPurchasesStillLoad`, by value.
- **ShipStation's page names money plainly, and what escapes the rule is a *terse* flow rather than a page that hides it.** The article SONNY-506 was filed from was read in a browser on 2026-09-17 (`help.shipstation.com/hc/en-us/articles/360026156831-Create-Print-Your-First-Label`, title verified): its own words are "Click the **Create + Print Label** button", and then "you'll be prompted here to add your label payment method and add funds to the balance used to purchase your labels." **A flow keeping that second sentence is refused today and was refused before this branch**, as `add + funds`. A flow that stops at the button is not, and cannot be — no list can know that ShipStation's "Create + Print Label" spends money and Trello's "Create" does not. The pitfall this corrects is in the ticket's own framing: the page is not silent about money, the *summary of it* is, so what a pack lane owes is writing down where the flow leaves the user, not finding better words for the button. (The same read reproduced the `innerText` trap the marketing-growth entry records: 806 characters against `textContent`'s 11,357, with the steps in the collapsed part.)
- **Where a flow leaves the user is not checkable, so it is stated in the two places that can act on it rather than in a comment.** The planner block every pack is read under now says a skill never authorises spending the user's money — one sentence, and the only place a pack's guidance can be met with a rule at the moment it is read — and `theMoneyRuleCannotSeeAPurchaseTheStepsDoNotName` holds ShipStation's terse wording asserting that the rule does **not** refuse it, so that a later session reading the purchase words cannot conclude the class is covered. That test also asserts the header sentence, so removing the sentence removes the record of why it exists. Below both, the screen-control loop already classifies a buy as `affects_others`, which asks the user.
- **A list entry that can never fire is noise in a security rule, and five were removed after being measured.** "Confirm and pay" and "Top up" are already test 1's, on `pay` and `top up`; "Buy postage" and "Complete the purchase" are already reached by `buy` and `purchase`; "Add funds" is already test 2's, as `add + funds`. Each has a row in `aFlowThatEndsInAPurchaseDoesNotLoad` instead, which is what keeps that a measurement rather than an assumption — and what will say so if one stops holding.
- **The credential rule was widened on one sighting, and the fail-closed shape is the reason that was enough** (founders, 2026-09-17, recorded here so a later reader can judge it rather than inherit it). Pinterest's "Keep board secret" is the only collision recorded anywhere in this tree. What made one sighting sufficient is that the change cannot widen anything else: `secret` is excused only when **every** occurrence is **immediately** beside a listed word, and the other twelve collision-capable words keep refusing outright. The other direction was held by value rather than by argument — eight credential steps that must still refuse, including the two shapes a looser rule would have let through ("Paste the secret into the chat.", which names a chat but not beside the word, and "Keep the board secret, then paste the API secret below.", where one occurrence is excused and one is not). This is SONNY-492's answer to the same question in the trigger check: teach the check the words rather than make the packs avoid them.
- **The corpus that could have shown more credential collisions is censored by construction, and its zero reads like good news.** Running the rule over every field of every shipped pack finds **0 hits over 4306 fields** — and it must, because a pack carrying one does not load, so the lanes removed them before they reached the tree. The control that says the sweep can find something at all is the same matcher over the catalogue's uncensored cells (`docs/sonny-skill-sites.tsv`'s name, why-in-list, sign-in and doc-url columns, 2537 cells), which answers **8**, every one of them a genuine credential document — password resets, a 2FA article, a credentials-configuration page — and not one a collision. Known limitations / deferred scope:
- **The terse-purchase class is not closed and cannot be closed here.** A flow whose last step names an ordinary control on a screen that charges loads, and no check in this repository can see it. It is named in `SkillPackMoneyRule`'s doc comment, held by `theMoneyRuleCannotSeeAPurchaseTheStepsDoNotName`, and answered at run time by the planner block and the consequence classifier rather than at load time.
- **Purchase words are a proxy for a purchase, and the proxy is wrong in both directions.** A flow that buys without naming a purchase word loads (the limitation above); a flow that names one without buying is refused, which costs a pack a flow and surfaces loudly as a load failure rather than quietly.
- **The other collision-capable credential words keep refusing outright** — `passcode`, `passphrase`, `credential`, `otp`, `2fa`, `mfa`, the cased `PIN` and the rest of the fourteen. **SONNY-514** is the named landing spot, and it opens with nothing to do on purpose: it needs a measured sighting per word (founders, 2026-09-17). Recorded there with the population, including that 9 of the 11 `it_accounts_security` catalogue rows are still shallow and their interface vocabulary is credential vocabulary by definition.
