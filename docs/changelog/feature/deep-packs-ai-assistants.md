Architectural decisions / pitfalls discovered (required, write "none" if true):
- **A landed-URL check is not a uniform check, and this branch is where that was measured.** Comparing the URL a read comes back with against the URL asked for did catch a real defect here — `…/7925741-chatgpt-shared-links-faq` lands on `…/7925741-chatgpt-shared-links`, corrected in `8cfd8884` — but review-261 then measured *why* it caught it, and the mechanism does not generalise: on an id-authoritative help centre an entirely invented slug still serves the right article and rewrites the address bar to the canonical one (openai, deepl and jasper all do this), so a stale citation there is invisible to a URL comparison, while Perplexity's help centre answers "Page Not Found" instead. **The page's own title is the only check that is uniform**, and it is what catches the interesting case: `…/10354769-what-is-a-thread` renders a page titled "What is a Session?", because Perplexity renamed Threads to Sessions and kept the slug — a live URL, not a stale citation. Check the title; treat a landed-URL match as a weaker signal than it looks.
- **A redirect check read too early answers "it stayed put" and looks clean.** Review-261's probe of `app.base44.com/` ran 3 s after navigating and reported the origin unchanged with an empty body, while the tab was still travelling to `/login`; HeyGen took more than 13 s to finish redirecting. So a reading of where a page landed needs a settle window and is better taken from the tab than from a script in the page. This is the clean-zero family in `CLAUDE.md`'s Claims and evidence applied to time rather than to bytes: the wrong answer is the reassuring one.
- **A help centre can serve an empty article body to text extraction while plainly rendering the steps.** Jasper does it on every article — `get_page_text` returns only the "Get in touch" footer — and review-261 hit the same on Base44 and Synthesia. The steps are in the accessibility tree, which is the same page a person sees. A lane that trusts the extractor concludes the site documents nothing; a lane that trusts a scripted fetch writes flows from nothing and cannot tell.
- **Money hides in the neighbouring control, so read where a flow leaves the user rather than scanning its steps for money words** (SONNY-506; the validating test only sees words). Three calls on this branch, all confirmed by the reviewer against the pages: Grammarly starts at `account.grammarly.com/customize`, the Writing room, because the Account Hub's root puts a payment control and a password control one click from step 1; DeepL's file flow stops at Download and carries the per-language allowance as a note, not a step; Jasper's flows come from Changing Roles and Default Workspace Language because "Adding Team Members" is the front door to buying a seat on a full workspace.
- **A "clean profile" is a claim to check, not a property to assume.** The profile these pages were read in carried a live Google session and a live Claude session. Both were found by reading the page — an account chip in `support.google.com`'s header, a signed-in app landing on `claude.ai/` — rather than by inspecting the browser, and both sites were re-read after a signed-out restart: neither help centre personalises an article, every phrase the flows rest on was still present, and no flow changed. Gemini gives the clean before-and-after (the account chip becomes a Sign in control); `support.claude.com` renders no identity either way, so there the evidence is the content and saying so is more honest than implying a check that could not run.
- **When two measurements disagree, take a third rather than writing prose that lets both stand** (F5, and `CLAUDE.md`'s rule). The PR body said DeepL's file-translation article sits beside "Purchase additional file translations"; the reviewer measured the related list on that page and found "Allocate monthly file translations for team subscriptions" instead, and said plainly that not finding the former is not the same as its not existing. Both are real and the two readings were of different lists: the article's *Related articles* block names Allocate (`/Allocate monthly file translations/` → true on that page, `/Purchase additional/` → also true elsewhere on it), while the *File translation section sidebar* both articles carry links to `…/23411412877084-Purchase-additional-file-translations`, which resolves to a real article titled "Purchase additional file translations". The instrument was the difference, not the fact; name the list.
