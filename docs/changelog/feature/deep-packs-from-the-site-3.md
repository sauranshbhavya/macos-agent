**Three findings about the reader.**
- **A first read can come from the page before.** Right after navigating to `support.docusign.com`, a `find` answered by describing the RingCentral page the tab had just left. A read that waited for the new host was correct. The kickoff's navigate-wait-read-twice rule caught it.
- **A client-side redirect makes a read early, not stale.** RingCentral's article pages hold their own title for a few seconds before moving to search. A single read at about 2.5 s would have recorded a readable article that no visitor can actually stay on.
- **A zero at an index says nothing about the page one link below it.** Triage's "0 article links" for RingCentral was a true count of its index, while its articles sit behind the product hubs. A sort built on that count would have filed RingCentral with the help centres that have no links at all, and the actual barrier turned out to be different: articles that will not stay open.

**Whether the method works, updated for the next session.** Chatwork's Zendesk help centre became a pack within minutes, like Plaud's and Circle's on PR #271. Across both branches, the three Zendesk-style centres (Plaud, Circle, Chatwork) yielded, and every centre built as an app did not: GoTo's browse tree, DocuSign's and BILL's Salesforce communities, and RingCentral's self-redirecting articles. **That split is the most useful thing this group found**, and it can be seen in one navigation: an index whose article links are ordinary `href`s that stay put.
