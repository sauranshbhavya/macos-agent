Architectural decisions / pitfalls discovered (required, write "none" if true):

**Whether the method works — the statement SONNY-512 asks for, written for the session that takes the other thirty-four.** It works, and it is a different method from reading documentation rather than a substitute for it, with a far lower yield per hour. The deciding property is not whether a site has documentation. It is **whether the product renders anything at all to a visitor who is not signed in**, and that is knowable in one navigation.

Of the four that landed, only two are the method in its intended form. Grok's flow is written from `grok.com` itself, which signed out renders the whole prompt surface — the box reading *Ask Grok anything*, `Attach`, a mode control labelled `Fast`, `Submit`, with `Imagine` and `Settings` in the side navigation and `Sign in`/`Sign up` in the corner, and no password input anywhere. OpenRouter's is written from `openrouter.ai/models`, which signed out renders the full catalogue: a `Search models…` box, two dropdowns and five checkboxes, `List view`/`Table view` tabs, `Pinned`, and `Discover Models`. The other two — Canva and Zoho Desk — came from help centres that do carry step-level flows after all (`canva.com/help/…` and `help.zoho.com/portal/en/kb/desk/…`), so for those rows `site` records **how the pages were read**, in a browser, and not that the source was a different kind of page. That is worth stating plainly because the row's word is otherwise easy to read as a claim about the source.

**What stopped the other sixteen, in six shapes.** Every reading below was taken in this session, signed out, in a profile proved signed out first.

1. **The product is wholly behind sign-in, and the bare origin is an account page.** `chat.deepseek.com/` lands on `chat.deepseek.com/sign_in` with one `input[type=password]` and the line *"By signing up or logging in, you consent to DeepSeek's Terms of Use and Privacy Policy"* — the SONNY-510 shape exactly. `lucid.app/` lands on `lucid.app/users/login#/login`, title *Lucid visual collaboration suite: Log in*, heading *Sign up free or log in*, body carrying *By registering, yo…*. `colab.research.google.com/` renders the two words *Sign in* and nothing else. For these a signed-out reader has nothing, and no amount of care recovers it.
2. **The start page lands on another host.** `desk.zoho.com/` redirects to `www.zoho.com/desk/?zsrc=fromproduct` — the marketing page, on a different host from the pack's domain. `business.whatsapp.com/` redirects to `whatsappbusiness.com/`, a different registrable domain. `platform.openai.com/docs/quickstart` redirects to `developers.openai.com/api/docs/quickstart`. The host check on a flow's `startURL` passes in every one of these, correctly, and says nothing about where the URL lands — which is SONNY-510's finding reaching three more sites.
3. **A bot check stands in front of the pages.** `codesandbox.io/docs` answers *"Just a moment… codesandbox.io Performing security verification"*. That is not a page to work around: completing or bypassing bot detection is forbidden, so the site is simply unreadable this way.
4. **The founders' credential rule removes the site's own first task.** For a developer platform the first thing a person does is create an API key, and `SkillPackCredentialRule` refuses that phrase — rightly. OpenAI Platform's quickstart is that task and almost nothing else, and everything past it (dashboard, playground, usage) is behind sign-in. So `openai_platform` cannot be deep on evidence a signed-out reader can take, and that is the rule working rather than a gap.
5. **A support centre whose article links are not links.** `support.docusign.com` is a Salesforce community: its index renders article titles, `read_page` with the interactive filter returns twelve controls and no article hrefs, and the article ids are opaque. Docusign's own product pages carry benefit copy and no steps — a sweep of every `h2`, `h3` and `li` on `docusign.com/products/electronic-signature` returns 45 headings and not one step.
6. **Not reached** — chatwork, circle, goto_meeting, ringcentral, ironclad, bill, plaud, google_ai_studio. Browsing time ran out before them; nothing is known about them either way, which is different from the five shapes above and is recorded as such on the ticket.

**Four findings about the reader rather than about the sites**, each of which cost time here and will cost it again:

- **`javascript_tool` is refused outright on some pages**, with `[BLOCKED: Cookie/query string data]`. Reproduced on a page whose own URL carries a query — on `support.docusign.com/s/?language=en_US` even `document.title + document.querySelectorAll('a').length` alone was refused — and on pages whose returned text carries consent-banner wording. `read_page` and `get_page_text` work on those same pages, so **a blocked script is not an unreadable page**, and recording one as the other is the clean-zero family arriving through the tool rather than the site.
- **`get_page_text` prioritises article content and will hand back a blog teaser.** On `docusign.com/products/electronic-signature` it returned three sentences from an `<article>` card promoting a blog post, while the page's own heading sweep returned 45 headings. Its output looks like a complete reading of a thin page. It is not.
- **Inside `browser_batch`, the first navigation to a domain the extension has not seen fails** — *"Navigation to this domain is not allowed"* — and the same URL navigated standalone succeeds. Measured twice, on `openrouter.ai/docs` and on `www.zoho.com/desk/`, both refused as a batch's second item and both fine alone. So a batch is for pages on a domain already opened; a new site's first page is a standalone call.
- **The wait-and-retry rule paid for itself once in twenty sites.** `help.zoho.com/portal/en/kb/desk` read about four seconds after landing gave title *Zoho Corporation* and **0 anchors**; the same page about nine seconds in gave title *Desk Knowledge Base*, 145 anchors and 2302 characters of text. The first read is indistinguishable from an unreadable page, and Zoho Desk is one of this branch's four deep packs only because it was read twice.

**What the next session should do differently, concretely.** Triage before reading. Open only the bare product origin for each of the thirty-four, record the landed title, the landed host and whether the page carries a password input — one navigation each — and sort the site into *renders the product*, *help centre on its own domain*, or *sign-in wall*, before any reading time is spent on it. That one cheap pass would have sorted this group in a few minutes and left the whole budget for the sites that can actually yield. Reading was attempted here in catalogue order instead, and most of the cost went to sites that could never have produced a flow.

Five of them are named above with a measured reason and are unlikely to become deep by this method at all; eight were simply not reached and are the next session's first work, after the triage pass.
