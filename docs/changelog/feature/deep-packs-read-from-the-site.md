Architectural decisions / pitfalls discovered (required, write "none" if true):

**Whether the method works — the statement SONNY-512 asks for, written for the session that takes the other thirty-four.** It works, and it is a different method from reading documentation rather than a substitute for it, with a far lower yield per hour. The deciding property is not whether a site has documentation. It is **whether the product renders anything at all to a visitor who is not signed in**, and that is knowable in one navigation.

Of the four that landed, only two are the method in its intended form. Grok's flow is written from `grok.com` itself, which signed out renders the whole prompt surface — the box reading *Ask Grok anything*, `Attach`, a mode control labelled `Fast`, `Submit`, with `Imagine` and `Settings` in the side navigation and `Sign in`/`Sign up` in the corner, and no password input anywhere. OpenRouter's is written from `openrouter.ai/models`, which signed out renders the full catalogue: a `Search models…` box, two dropdowns and five checkboxes, `List view`/`Table view` tabs, `Pinned`, and `Discover Models`. The other two — Canva and Zoho Desk — came from help centres that do carry step-level flows after all (`canva.com/help/…` and `help.zoho.com/portal/en/kb/desk/…`), so for those rows `site` records **how the pages were read**, in a browser, and not that the source was a different kind of page. That is worth stating plainly because the row's word is otherwise easy to read as a claim about the source.

**What stopped the other sixteen, in six shapes.** Every reading below was taken in this session, signed out, in a profile proved signed out first.

1. **The product is wholly behind sign-in, and the bare origin is an account page.** `chat.deepseek.com/` lands on `chat.deepseek.com/sign_in` with one `input[type=password]` and the line *"By signing up or logging in, you consent to DeepSeek's Terms of Use and Privacy Policy"* — the SONNY-510 shape exactly. `lucid.app/` lands on `lucid.app/users/login#/login`, title *Lucid visual collaboration suite: Log in*, heading *Sign up free or log in*, body carrying *By registering, yo…*. `colab.research.google.com/` renders the two words *Sign in* and nothing else. For these a signed-out reader has nothing, and no amount of care recovers it.
2. **The start page lands on another host.** `desk.zoho.com/` redirects to `www.zoho.com/desk/?zsrc=fromproduct` — the marketing page, on a different host from the pack's domain. `business.whatsapp.com/` redirects to `whatsappbusiness.com/`, a different registrable domain. `platform.openai.com/docs/quickstart` redirects to `developers.openai.com/api/docs/quickstart`. The host check on a flow's `startURL` passes in every one of these, correctly, and says nothing about where the URL lands — which is SONNY-510's finding reaching three more sites.
3. **A bot check stands in front of the pages.** `codesandbox.io/docs` answers *"Just a moment… codesandbox.io Performing security verification"*. That is not a page to work around: completing or bypassing bot detection is forbidden, so the site is simply unreadable this way.
4. **The founders' credential rule removes the site's own first task.** For a developer platform the first thing a person does is create an API key, and `SkillPackCredentialRule` refuses that phrase — rightly. OpenAI Platform's quickstart is that task and almost nothing else, and everything past it (dashboard, playground, usage) is behind sign-in. So `openai_platform` cannot be deep on evidence a signed-out reader can take, and that is the rule working rather than a gap.
5. **A support centre whose article links are not links.** `support.docusign.com` is a Salesforce community: its index renders article titles, `read_page` with the interactive filter returns twelve controls and no article hrefs, and the article ids are opaque. Docusign's own product pages carry benefit copy and no steps — a sweep of every `h2`, `h3` and `li` on `docusign.com/products/electronic-signature` returns 45 headings and not one step.
6. **Not read for flows** — chatwork, circle, goto_meeting, ringcentral, ironclad, bill, plaud, google_ai_studio. Reading time ran out before them. The triage pass that followed has since opened every one of their origins, so each now has a landed title, a landed host, a password-input count and the help index to start from; what none of them has is a flow. `chatwork` is the one that looks likely to join the settled list — `chatwork.com/` lands on `auth.chatwork.com/u/login/identifier` with a password input — but its help centre has not been looked at, so it is not claimed here.

**Four findings about the reader rather than about the sites**, each of which cost time here and will cost it again:

- **`javascript_tool` is refused outright on some pages**, with `[BLOCKED: Cookie/query string data]`. Reproduced on a page whose own URL carries a query — on `support.docusign.com/s/?language=en_US` even `document.title + document.querySelectorAll('a').length` alone was refused — and on pages whose returned text carries consent-banner wording. `read_page` and `get_page_text` work on those same pages, so **a blocked script is not an unreadable page**, and recording one as the other is the clean-zero family arriving through the tool rather than the site.
- **`get_page_text` prioritises article content and will hand back a blog teaser.** On `docusign.com/products/electronic-signature` it returned three sentences from an `<article>` card promoting a blog post, while the page's own heading sweep returned 45 headings. Its output looks like a complete reading of a thin page. It is not.
- **Inside `browser_batch`, the first navigation to a domain the extension has not seen fails** — *"Navigation to this domain is not allowed"* — and the same URL navigated standalone succeeds. Measured twice, on `openrouter.ai/docs` and on `www.zoho.com/desk/`, both refused as a batch's second item and both fine alone. So a batch is for pages on a domain already opened; a new site's first page is a standalone call.
- **The wait-and-retry rule paid for itself once in twenty sites.** `help.zoho.com/portal/en/kb/desk` read about four seconds after landing gave title *Zoho Corporation* and **0 anchors**; the same page about nine seconds in gave title *Desk Knowledge Base*, 145 anchors and 2302 characters of text. The first read is indistinguishable from an unreadable page, and Zoho Desk is one of this branch's four deep packs only because it was read twice.

**What the next session should do differently, concretely.** Triage before reading. Open only the bare product origin for each of the thirty-four, record the landed title, the landed host and whether the page carries a password input — one navigation each — and sort the site into *renders the product*, *help centre on its own domain*, or *sign-in wall*, before any reading time is spent on it. That one cheap pass would have sorted this group in a few minutes and left the whole budget for the sites that can actually yield. Reading was attempted here in catalogue order instead, and most of the cost went to sites that could never have produced a flow.

**The founder decision this branch produced, recorded 2026-09-17 on SONNY-512: the goal is every pack that *can be read* is deep, not that every pack is deep.** A site whose product shows nothing to a signed-out visitor stays shallow with its reason recorded against that row. This amends the 2026-09-16 decision ("every skill pack is deep, all 473 of them") in one direction only — it does not lower the bar for a pack that can be read, and it does not let a row go shallow without a measured reason. It exists because this branch measured five sites where no evidence a signed-out reader can take would ever make the pack deep — a sixth, Zoho Calendar, was found in the triage pass afterwards — and a target of 473 would have been met by writing flows nobody had read.

**The triage, and why it is the shape of this work rather than an aside.** All 54 `site` rows were opened at the bare origin of their own domain, signed out, one navigation each, and the landed title, landed host and password-input count recorded. The table is on SONNY-512. Four numbers out of it: **7** of the 54 land on a page carrying a password input; **6** land on a different host or a different domain from the one the row names (`whatsapp_business`, `zoho_desk`, `zoho_calendar`, `zoho_crm`, `zoho_forms`, `openai_platform`); **6** render the product or a real signed-out page rather than marketing (`grok`, `openrouter`, `google_ai_studio`, `eventbrite`, `indeed`, `amazon_seller_central`); and **43 name at least one help, docs or academy link reachable from their own origin** (3 name none, 8 not probed). The pass took a small fraction of the session that read in catalogue order and produced more usable direction than it did, which is the practical lesson: **triage the population before reading any of it.**

**Whether the catalogue is what is wrong about Canva and Zoho Desk — yes about the research behind their rows, no about the value the rows carry.** Two claims were folded together when these 54 rows were grouped: SONNY-461's research finding that the site's public documentation carries no step-level flows, and SONNY-501's column value `site` meaning the flows were read off the live site in a browser. The first is **false** for both, measured: `canva.com/help/use-templates/` is Canva's own public help article carrying a numbered click path, and `help.zoho.com/portal/en/kb/desk/…/merging-tickets` is Zoho's own knowledge base reading "Navigate to the Tickets module. Select the Department. …". The second is **true** for both. So the rows are correctly valued and were incorrectly grouped, and nothing about the packs is unusual. The 43-of-54 figure above says where else the premise could have been checked and was not — a count of links is not a claim about what those help centres contain, and two contradictions out of two checks is a small sample, but it is the wrong direction for a premise that removed 54 sites from deep-pack work.

**The open question that follows, stated because a future session will hit it.** If `deep` and `site` are meant to distinguish the *kind* of evidence rather than the act of reading, the workable definition is: `deep` means the flows came from a documentation page anyone can re-open at a cited URL, and `site` means they came from the running product, where no artifact exists. Under that definition this branch's four rows split — Canva and Zoho Desk become `deep`, Grok and OpenRouter stay `site` — and that is also the reasoning review-260's F4 used when it made the `doc_url` cells mandatory for `site` rows. The change is not made here: it is a founders' decision, it moves a tally `theCommittedCatalogueIsTheListTheFoundersDecided` pins by value, and all four rows are true as they stand under the doc comment's current wording.

**A sixth row is settled by its start page rather than by its pages, which is a shape nothing else in this group had.** `zoho_calendar`'s flows are readable and were read — `www.zoho.com/calendar/help/create-and-edit-events.html`, "Create and edit event | Zoho Calendar", carries three named methods with steps for each. What stops it is that `https://calendar.zoho.com/` lands on `https://www.zoho.com/calendar/`, another host, whose page carries **1 `input[type=password]`** in an inline sign-up form — and SONNY-510's rule is that a landed page carrying a password field is not a start page. Every legal start URL for that pack goes through it, and a deeper path on `calendar.zoho.com` could not be established without guessing one. The same holds for `zoho_crm`, `zoho_forms` and `bigin` in the other 34. **`zoho_desk` is the exception and was re-checked deliberately after this was found**: `desk.zoho.com/` → `www.zoho.com/desk/` reads PW=0, 3 forms, 0 sign-up headings and no registration line, so the pack that ships on this branch is not in the trap.

Known limitations / deferred scope: sixteen of the twenty sites are still shallow. **Six are now settled with a measured reason** — DeepSeek, Lucid, Google Colab and CodeSandbox by their own pages, OpenAI Platform by this repository's credential rule, and Zoho Calendar by its start page — each named individually on SONNY-512 with what was tried. The other ten (docusign, ironclad, bill, whatsapp_business, ringcentral, circle, chatwork, goto_meeting, google_ai_studio, plaud) are unread rather than unreadable, and the triage table says which index each should be started from. No conversion was begun that could not be finished and verified in this session: Circle's knowledge base, the one attempted, is still an index three levels down (`help.circle.so` → `/p/basics` → `/p/basics/getting-started`), and starting a fifth pack there would have left it half-written.

Open questions (required, write "none" if true):
- Whether a row that no evidence can make deep should say so in the catalogue — a fourth value, or the `shallow` the column still legally holds. The founders' decision of 2026-09-17 settles the *policy* (such a row stays shallow with its reason recorded) but not where the reason lives; today it lives only on SONNY-512.
- Whether `deep` and `site` should be redefined as documentation-versus-product rather than by who read it, which would move Canva and Zoho Desk to `deep`. Recommended above; not taken here.
- Whether a flow may start at the product's front door when a signed-out visitor is redirected to a marketing page carrying a sign-up form. A signed-in user — who every flow is written for — never sees that page. A yes makes zoho_calendar, zoho_crm, zoho_forms and bigin writable at once, and Zoho Calendar's flows are already read and recorded on the ticket.
