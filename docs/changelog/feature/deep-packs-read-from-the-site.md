Architectural decisions / pitfalls discovered (required, write "none" if true):

**Whether the method works — the statement SONNY-512 asks for, written for the session that takes the other thirty-four.** It works, and it is a different method from reading documentation rather than a substitute for it, with a far lower yield per hour. The deciding property is not whether a site has documentation. It is **whether the product renders anything at all to a visitor who is not signed in**, and that is knowable in one navigation.

Of the four that landed, only two are the method in its intended form, and those two keep the `site` value. Grok's flows are written from `grok.com` and `grok.com/imagine`, which signed out render the whole prompt surface. OpenRouter's is written from `openrouter.ai/models`, which signed out renders the full catalogue. Canva's and Zoho Desk's came from help centres that carry step-level flows after all (`canva.com/help/…` and `help.zoho.com/portal/en/kb/desk/…`), and those two rows are `deep` — see the founders' redefinition below.

**Three of those flows contradicted their own pages, and review-264 caught all three.** Recorded here rather than quietly fixed, because each is a different way of being wrong about a page you have actually opened. **Grok**: the flow said *Imagine and Settings sit in the side navigation*, and `grok.com` has no side navigation at all — `nav`, `aside` and `[role=navigation]` each answer **0**, and `Imagine`, `Settings`, `Sign in` and `Sign up` are one row at the top right, measured at x=1570, 1690, 1738 and 1823 with y=11 for all four. The `sections` list claimed `Home`, which was only the logo's accessible name (`Home page`, x=20 y=12). Re-read: the real pair is `Chat` and `Imagine`, each the accessible name of a link the other page carries, and the `Fast` control's own accessible name is **Model select** with `aria-haspopup=menu` — so the rewritten step says what it opens instead of guessing what it does. The re-read also found that `grok.com/imagine` renders signed out once given time to settle, so Grok now has a second flow rather than one. **OpenRouter**: the flow started at `/models` while its first step told the planner to go to the home page and click Models, and the manual-test row asserted the home-page version — a start page and a step that could not both be followed. The step now starts where the start page points, and the manual-test row checks that it does not pass through the home page. **Canva**: the entry claimed `www.canva.com/` was a "good start page". It is not, by this branch's own test — on the signed-out landing page `/custom size/i` over `document.documentElement.innerHTML` is **false** and the page carries **0** inputs of any kind, so neither flow's first control is there. The help articles describe the *signed-in* homepage. Both flows now say so in their first step, and the manual-test row checks the plan carries that caveat.

**What stopped the other sixteen, in six shapes.** Every reading below was taken in this session, signed out, in a profile proved signed out first.

1. **The product is wholly behind sign-in, and the bare origin is an account page.** `chat.deepseek.com/` lands on `chat.deepseek.com/sign_in` with one `input[type=password]` and the line *"By signing up or logging in, you consent to DeepSeek's Terms of Use and Privacy Policy"* — the SONNY-510 shape exactly. `lucid.app/` lands on `lucid.app/users/login#/login`, title *Lucid visual collaboration suite: Log in*, heading *Sign up free or log in*, body carrying *By registering, yo…*. `colab.research.google.com/` renders the two words *Sign in* and nothing else. For these a signed-out reader has nothing, and no amount of care recovers it.
2. **The start page lands on another host.** `desk.zoho.com/` redirects to `www.zoho.com/desk/?zsrc=fromproduct` — the marketing page, on a different host from the pack's domain. `business.whatsapp.com/` redirects to `whatsappbusiness.com/`, a different registrable domain. `platform.openai.com/docs/quickstart` redirects to `developers.openai.com/api/docs/quickstart`. The host check on a flow's `startURL` passes in every one of these, correctly, and says nothing about where the URL lands — which is SONNY-510's finding reaching three more sites.
3. **A bot check stands in front of the pages.** `codesandbox.io/docs` answers *"Just a moment… codesandbox.io Performing security verification"*. That is not a page to work around: completing or bypassing bot detection is forbidden, so the site is simply unreadable this way.
4. **The founders' credential rule removes the site's own first task.** For a developer platform the first thing a person does is create an API key, and `SkillPackCredentialRule` refuses that phrase — rightly. OpenAI Platform's quickstart is that task and almost nothing else, and everything past it (dashboard, playground, usage) is behind sign-in. So `openai_platform` cannot be deep on evidence a signed-out reader can take, and that is the rule working rather than a gap.
5. **A support centre whose article links are not links.** `support.docusign.com` is a Salesforce community: its index renders article titles, `read_page` with the interactive filter returns twelve controls and no article hrefs, and the article ids are opaque. Docusign's own product pages carry benefit copy and no steps — a sweep of every `h2`, `h3` and `li` on `docusign.com/products/electronic-signature` returns 45 headings and not one step.
6. **Not read for flows** — chatwork, circle, goto_meeting, ringcentral, ironclad, bill, plaud, google_ai_studio. Reading time ran out before them. The triage pass that followed has since opened every one of their origins, so each now has a landed title, a landed host, a password-input count and the help index to start from; what none of them has is a flow. `chatwork` is the one that looks likely to join the settled list — `chatwork.com/` lands on `auth.chatwork.com/u/login/identifier` with a password input — but its help centre has not been looked at, so it is not claimed here.

**Four findings about the reader rather than about the sites**, each of which cost time here and will cost it again:

- **`javascript_tool` is refused outright on some pages**, with `[BLOCKED: Cookie/query string data]`. Reproduced on a page whose own URL carries a query — on `support.docusign.com/s/?language=en_US` even `document.title + document.querySelectorAll('a').length` alone was refused — and on pages whose returned text carries consent-banner wording. `read_page` and `get_page_text` work on those same pages, so **a blocked script is not an unreadable page**, and recording one as the other is the clean-zero family arriving through the tool rather than the site.
- **`get_page_text` prioritises article content and will hand back a blog teaser.** On `docusign.com/products/electronic-signature` it returned three sentences from an `<article>` card promoting a blog post, while the page's own heading sweep returned 45 headings. Its output looks like a complete reading of a thin page. It is not.
- **Inside `browser_batch`, the first navigation to a domain the extension has not seen fails** — *"Navigation to this domain is not allowed"* — and the same URL navigated standalone succeeds. Measured twice, on `openrouter.ai/docs` and on `www.zoho.com/desk/`, both refused as a batch's second item and both fine alone. So a batch is for pages on a domain already opened; a new site's first page is a standalone call.
- **The wait-and-retry rule paid for itself once in twenty sites.** `help.zoho.com/portal/en/kb/desk` read about four seconds after landing gave title *Zoho Corporation* and **0 anchors**; the same page about nine seconds in gave title *Desk Knowledge Base*, 145 anchors and 2302 characters of text. The first read is indistinguishable from an unreadable page, and Zoho Desk is one of this branch's four deep packs only because it was read twice.

**What the next session should do differently, concretely.** Triage before reading. Open only the bare product origin for each of the thirty-four, record the landed title, the landed host and whether the page carries a password input — one navigation each — and sort the site into *renders the product*, *help centre on its own domain*, or *sign-in wall*, before any reading time is spent on it. That one cheap pass would have sorted this group in a few minutes and left the whole budget for the sites that can actually yield. Reading was attempted here in catalogue order instead, and most of the cost went to sites that could never have produced a flow.

**The founder decision this branch produced, recorded 2026-09-17 on SONNY-512: the goal is every pack that *can be read* is deep, not that every pack is deep.** A site whose product shows nothing to a signed-out visitor stays shallow with its reason recorded against that row. This amends the 2026-09-16 decision ("every skill pack is deep, all 473 of them") in one direction only — it does not lower the bar for a pack that can be read, and it does not let a row go shallow without a measured reason. It exists because this branch measured five sites where no evidence a signed-out reader can take would ever make the pack deep — a sixth, Zoho Calendar, was found in the triage pass afterwards — and a target of 473 would have been met by writing flows nobody had read.

**The triage, its first version, and why that version did not hold.** All 54 `site` rows were opened at the bare origin of their own domain, signed out, and the landed title, landed host and password-input count recorded. Review-264 then ran that specification unchanged on three sites and **it got two of them wrong, in opposite directions**: `aistudio.google.com` lands on `/welcome`, same host, 0 password inputs, and so sorted as "renders the product" when it is a marketing page; `calendar.zoho.com` lands on another host with a password input, and so sorted as "sign-in wall" when it is a marketing page carrying a sign-up form — and it is the one site the entry predicted would yield. A sorter wrong in both directions at once is not a sorter, so **the first table's buckets are withdrawn**.

**What the second version changes, and the evidence that it holds.** Four things, each answering one of the failures. A **settle poll** replaces the fixed wait — up to 25 × 400 ms until `document.readyState` is `complete` and the page has more than six controls — which is also what made `docusign.com` probe at all, where the first version timed out at 45 s twice. A **product-control probe replaces the password signal**: the question is no longer "does this page have a password field" but "is there a control a visitor could drive the product with" — a `textarea`, a `contenteditable`, a search box, a grid or a listbox, excluding anything inside a form that also holds an email or password input, anything inside a cookie banner, header, nav or footer, and any input whose accessible name is a site search. A **fourth bucket** joins product and sign-in: `marketing`, and `unsorted` as the residue, so a page the probe cannot sort says so instead of being forced into a bucket. And the landed **path** is read first, so an auth route sorts as sign-in whatever else is on the page. The rule is: `product` when a driver is present and no call-to-action is; `signin` when the landed path is an auth route or an auth control is present with no call-to-action; `marketing` when a call-to-action is present; `unsorted` otherwise.

**Both of review-264's counter-examples now sort correctly, and the two real product pages still sort as product** — which is the control, because a rule that can only say "marketing" has not been tested. `aistudio.google.com/welcome` → drv=0, cta=4 → marketing. `calendar.zoho.com` → `www.zoho.com/calendar/`, drv=0, pw=1, cta=1 → marketing. `grok.com` → drv=1, cta=0 → product. `openrouter.ai/models` → drv=1 (`Search models...`), cta=0 → product. `platform.openai.com/` → `/login`, which the path test catches before its call-to-action can sort it as marketing.

**The second version was re-run over this branch's own 20 rows and the table is on SONNY-512.** The other 34 belong to other tickets and are **not** re-sorted here: their first-version buckets are withdrawn rather than corrected, and the session that takes them runs the probe as specified above. Saying which rows were re-measured and which were not is the point — a table that mixes the two reads as one measurement.

**What the re-run found that the first version had wrong beyond those two.** `codesandbox.io`'s **origin** loads normally (title *CodeSandbox: Instant Cloud Development Environments*); the Cloudflare interstitial was on `/docs`, so "bot check" was a fact about its documentation route and not about the site — which is exactly what the documentation-route navigation was added to surface. And `deepseek.com` now carries a live prompt box on its marketing page (a `textarea` whose placeholder is *Ask anything, explore together*, class `hero-input`), so the origin sorts as `product`; whether it can be submitted signed out is unknown, because establishing that would mean typing, which this session does not do anywhere.

**The documentation-route navigation, which is the other half of the fix**, opens the first help or docs link found on the origin and records what it lands on. It separates a help centre that can be read from one that cannot, and that distinction decides a row's future more than its origin does: `help.circle.so` answers *Circle Knowledge Base* with 13 article links, `support.plaud.ai/hc/en-us` answers *Plaud* with 24 — both readable — while `support.ringcentral.com` (141 anchors), `support.goto.com` (89) and `help.bill.com/direct/s/` (6) each expose **0** article links, and `support.docusign.com` exposes twelve controls and no article link at all. Four help centres that all look present from the origin, and only two of them are reachable by a reader who cannot guess URLs.

**The founders redefined the two values on 2026-09-17, and this branch carries the change.** `deep` now means the flows came from a documentation page anybody can re-open at the URL the row cites; `site` means they came from the running product, where no artifact exists and the row's `doc_url` cells are the only record. The value names the kind of evidence, not who read it or how — a documentation page is read in a browser like everything else, and the reading rule that governs both stays in `CLAUDE.md`. **Three things moved together in one commit**, because a rule with two copies is a rule with one stale copy: the two rows, the tally, and every live statement of the pair. The sweep found exactly three — `SkillPack.swift`'s depth doc comment (the one a pack lane reads), `SkillPackTests`' `evidenceForADeepPack` doc comment, and the tally's own comment — plus `CLAUDE.md`'s clean-zero bullet, which stated a condition for `site` and now states the reading rule for both values and points at `SkillPack.swift` for the definition. `git grep -nE 'read off the live site|live site, read in a browser'` answers nothing under `Sources`, `Tests` or `CLAUDE.md` after the change.

**Canva and Zoho Desk are `deep` now; the other 52 are not re-classified.** Two sites measured is a small sample, and the 43-of-54 figure below is a count of links rather than a claim about what those pages contain. **A row moves one at a time, when somebody opens that site's documentation and finds flows in it** — never in bulk from the link count. That sentence is in `SkillPack.swift` too, where a pack lane will meet it.

**Whether the catalogue was what was wrong about Canva and Zoho Desk — yes about the research behind their rows, no about the value the rows carried.** Two claims were folded together when these 54 rows were grouped: SONNY-461's research finding that the site's public documentation carries no step-level flows, and SONNY-501's column value `site` meaning the flows were read off the live site in a browser. The first is **false** for both, measured: `canva.com/help/use-templates/` is Canva's own public help article carrying a numbered click path, and `help.zoho.com/portal/en/kb/desk/…/merging-tickets` is Zoho's own knowledge base reading "Navigate to the Tickets module. Select the Department. …". The second is **true** for both. So the rows are correctly valued and were incorrectly grouped, and nothing about the packs is unusual. The 43-of-54 figure above says where else the premise could have been checked and was not — a count of links is not a claim about what those help centres contain, and two contradictions out of two checks is a small sample, but it is the wrong direction for a premise that removed 54 sites from deep-pack work.

**What the redefinition cost to make, measured.** Two catalogue cells and one numeral: the tally in `theCommittedCatalogueIsTheListTheFoundersDecided` moves from `["deep": 419, "site": 54]` to `["deep": 421, "site": 52]`. The control is the half that matters — with both rows moved and the tally left alone, that test fails at `SkillPackTests.swift:95` with `(… → ["deep": 421, "site": 52]) == ["deep": 419, "site": 54]`, exit 1, 30 tests in 1 suite; with the tally corrected, exit 0 and 45 tests in 3 suites. So the tally is load-bearing rather than decorative, which is the thing a value table has to prove about itself.

**A sixth row is settled by its start page rather than by its pages, which is a shape nothing else in this group had.** `zoho_calendar`'s flows are readable and were read — `www.zoho.com/calendar/help/create-and-edit-events.html`, "Create and edit event | Zoho Calendar", carries three named methods with steps for each. What stops it is that `https://calendar.zoho.com/` lands on `https://www.zoho.com/calendar/`, another host, whose page carries **1 `input[type=password]`** in an inline sign-up form — and SONNY-510's rule is that a landed page carrying a password field is not a start page. Every legal start URL for that pack goes through it, and a deeper path on `calendar.zoho.com` could not be established without guessing one. The same holds for `zoho_crm`, `zoho_forms` and `bigin` in the other 34. **`zoho_desk` is the exception and was re-checked deliberately after this was found**: `desk.zoho.com/` → `www.zoho.com/desk/` reads PW=0, 3 forms, 0 sign-up headings and no registration line, so the pack that ships on this branch is not in the trap.
