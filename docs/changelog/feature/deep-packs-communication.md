Architectural decisions / pitfalls discovered (required, write "none" if true):
- **A start page that reads as healthy signed in can be a different site signed out, and only one of the two states is the one Sonny meets.** `https://meet.google.com/` signed out leaves the pack's own domain entirely and lands on `workspace.google.com/products/meet/` — a marketing page with **zero** controls matching "New meeting" — while the flow's first step says to go to Google Meet and click New meeting. Signed in, the same URL lands on `meet.google.com/home`, the product, and **nothing in the signed-in reading hints at the other**. The fix is `https://meet.google.com/landing`, which redirects to Google's own sign-in with `continue` and `followup` both set back to it: the product's own sign-in, returning to the product, on the pack's own host. The convention already existed in the catalogue — `google_voice`'s `sign_in_url` is `voice.google.com/landing`. **So a start-page check has to state which signed-in state it was taken in and prove it**; this branch's first sweep claimed "opened signed out" without establishing it, was in fact taken in a profile still signed in to Google, and got exactly the one site wrong where the two states differ. Five of the six were unaffected, which is luck rather than method.
- **The host rule cannot see any of this, and that is the general shape.** `SkillPackDecoder.decodeFlow` checks only that the start page's host is the pack's domain or a subdomain, so `streamyard.com/` — whose first interactive control creates an account and accepts terms, and which does **not** redirect — satisfies it exactly as the real app entry does. A check that follows redirects finds nothing wrong there, because nothing moved. Four shapes of this defect were found by four different reviewers on four different PRs within one day and are recorded together on **SONNY-510**; this branch fixes its own six and solves none of the class.
- **A self-hosted site has no start page that can be both on-domain and real, and that is a format limit rather than a missing URL.** Mattermost's three flows were written, verified against their pages and then **stood down to shallow**, with the catalogue row restored byte for byte. Mattermost is self-hosted, its `signInURL` is `null` for exactly that reason, and no URL on `mattermost.com` reaches a channel sidebar — so every flow began at a step unreachable from its own start page by construction. Keeping the flows and letting the first step carry the truth ("open your own team's address") was considered and rejected: the declared start page would still be a page the task cannot start on, neither the loader nor the validating test can see the difference, and `startURL` is read by the planner, so it would be a false statement shipped in data. A flow whose first step is unreachable by construction is worth less than no flow. The three flows cost nothing to restore once the format can express a start page that is not a URL.
- **Money hides in the neighbouring control, and the content rule is the wrong instrument for it.** Dialpad's *Add & Remove Team Members* page ends its add-a-user path at "Confirm any billing changes and add the user(s)". A flow built from that page **would have loaded** — no money *object* sits beside those verbs — so the rule sees nothing. That path was left out by hand and the pack ships *Restore a deleted team member* from the same page instead; the unresolved question is on **SONNY-506**. The rule did earn its keep in the other direction on this branch: it refused `movesMoney(field: "flows[1]", words: "withdraw")` on a Discord step whose meaning had nothing to do with money, which is a false refusal by meaning and the right one by rule.
- **A browser reading can return the previous page, or a page that has not finished redirecting, and neither says so.** Three times on this branch a `navigate` reported success while the read that followed returned the *previous* page's text under the *previous* URL. Discord is the sharp case in the other direction: its redirect is client-side and had **not** settled after 13 seconds of waiting — the scripted read still reported `/channels/@me` with an empty body while a screenshot showed the real `/login` page underneath, so an instrument trusting the first read reports a healthy start page as a bare origin. The check that catches both: compare the URL the read reports against the URL asked for, every time, and treat a mismatch as no reading at all.
- **A cell-by-cell catalogue comparison is only as good as its base, and a stale base produces findings that belong to another branch.** Re-run after this branch updated from `main`, the same comparison against the original cut point `3bf70677` reports 14 rows and 40 cells with 23 findings — `carrd`, `framer`, `ghost`, `make`, `n8n`, `shipstation`, `wix` and `zapier`, every one of them SONNY-504's, arriving through `main`. Against `origin/main` it reports 6 rows and 17 cells and no findings. Both runs are correct about the question they were asked; only one of them is the question.
