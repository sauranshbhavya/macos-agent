Architectural decisions / pitfalls discovered (required, write "none" if true):
- **What held FreshBooks on 2026-09-18 is not known, and this entry first said it was.** Measured: `my.freshbooks.com/` stayed on "Checking your browser – reCAPTCHA" across two reads on 2026-09-18, at about 20:48 EDT, two minutes after `uptime` read a one-minute load average of 91.08, and those readings did not record whether the window was drawn. On 2026-09-19 the same page cleared by itself, with nothing pressed, on arrivals made with the window drawn (02:29 and 10:30), and landed on an ordinary sign-in, so the start-page verdict — `sign-in` — rests on those. This entry then explained the first hold as the undrawn window. **That was inferred, not shown, and the second review disproved the inference:** FreshBooks sets pass cookies that let a later arrival skip the check entirely, and with those two cookies expired before each of three fresh arrivals, the check cleared by itself on two *undrawn* arrivals as well as the drawn one. So an undrawn window does not hold this page, and a clearing arrival after an earlier one may have skipped the check rather than passed it. The load at the first reading is a candidate cause and is not shown either.
- **A tab can be undrawn with its window on screen, and a screenshot does not say so.** On 2026-09-19 Claude's tab was one of eleven in a Chrome window other sessions also use; only a window's active tab is painted, so `document.visibilityState` read `"hidden"` and a one-second `requestAnimationFrame` count read 0 while screenshots still captured the page. By founder choice the tab was made the window's active tab through AppleScript, by the tab id the automation tool uses (AppleScript's `id of tab` is the same number), before every read, after which the same checks read `"visible"` and 101 frames; every reading in the first review round carries that `visibilityState` beside it. The cost is the other sessions' tabs in that window going unpainted while this one reads.
- **An empty heading on this branch now means no visible heading of any kind, and two records needed changing to make that true.** The heading rule was corrected twice while this branch was open (SONNY-510: a visible `role="heading"` counts, then any of `h1`–`h6` does). Payoneer's first visible heading is an `h4 role="heading"` wrapping its Forgot password control. This branch had said its only heading was an `h3`, from queries that did not ask for an `h4`, which was wrong when written. GoCardless has two `h3`s, and which one is visible first depends on the window: at 1920 px wide the review saw the left promo panel's "Instant payments. Without card fees." first, and at 960 px that panel is `display: none` and the form's own "Sign in to your account" is the only one. The record names the form's `h3`, the one visible at every width. Stripe and Dext have no heading element of any kind and stay empty. A cookie banner's heading is never the page's (founders, 2026-09-18), which is why Toggl's record keeps its `h1` though a Cookiebot `role="heading"` precedes it.
- **A paid option one click into a flow is a paywall even when the flow's words name no money** (F1). Clockify's help page prints, directly under its create-a-project steps, that free-plan users can set only Public. Toggl's pricing page disagrees with itself in two panes. The visible annual card and full comparison table list "Project and task estimates" under Free and "Private projects and tasks" under paid plans. A hidden monthly pane lists "Project time estimates and alerts" under Starter, which is the pane the review read. Both readings are right about different panes, so the step drops estimates and stops at Private, which satisfies either. **The reading trap is reading every pane's text at once:** the first review read the page's whole text and so took the hidden pane's line for the page's, and it missed Private; the second review read the hidden Monthly pane on its own and found Private on paid plans there too, so both panes agree about Private and disagree only about estimates. FreshBooks documents its plan's client limit under the FAQs heading at the foot of its import page ("Why am I being asked to pay? If you try to import more clients than you have space for in your plan…"), expanded and on screen with nothing pressed — the second review read it drawn at 1920 px. This entry first called that answer collapsed and visible only to a DOM read; that came from reading `textContent` without checking whether the text was shown, and it was wrong.
- **The guards refuse the words a stop needs, and this branch hit it three ways (SONNY-536).** A stop at a paid option cannot say "upgrade" beside "plan", because the money rule's `purchaseControls` and `pricedWords` pair into a refusal, so Clockify's, Toggl's and FreshBooks' stops say "never change the plan". A stop before Wise's identity confirmation cannot say "password", because the credential rule refuses it anywhere, so it says the step asks the person to confirm their identity. Dext's stop cannot name the page's own "Purchase additional users" control, because `purchaseActs` refuses "purchase" alone, so it forbids changing "the plan, the user bundle or the number of users the plan allows". And the second round met a fourth: FreshBooks' Client Settings include "Charge Late Fees", and a stop naming it cannot be written because `charge` is on both of the money rule's lists (`actionVerbs` and `moneyObjects`), so the flow instead leaves every setting as it is and names none. Three lanes have now met this, which is why the ticket exists.
- **Four sites are held, each for a start-page reason.** Razorpay: `dashboard.razorpay.com/` lands on `accounts.razorpay.com/auth/`, titled "Razorpay Sign Up & Login - Access Your Dashboard", whose one field is headed "Get started with your email or phone number". On two arrivals the page had no sign-in, sign-up, create or register control anywhere, so the form is how an account gets created and there is no separate route. NetSuite: its sign-in page has no sign-up route, so it could pass only if netsuite.com offers no self-serve sign-up anywhere. It does: the homepage's "Test Drive NetSuite Next — Your free 3-day trial is here" leads to a page with a "Get my free test drive account" button. QuickBooks and Zoho Books: both sign in on hosts off their pack's site, and are SONNY-531's. QuickBooks also needs its row's domain decided, because both its product URLs are off `quickbooks.intuit.com`.
- **The money rule refuses a real control name, and the flow goes rather than the name.** Wise's team-member flow runs through "Team members and payment approvals" and Wave's add-a-customer flow through "Sales & Payments", so each pairs into `add + payment` or `remove + payment`. Both were dropped. Ramp's page noun "link" read as an action verb beside "Reimbursements", so that sentence was reworded.
- **Two help centres refuse `get_page_text`** (clockify.me and support.waveapps.com), and some `javascript_tool` output is withheld as query-string data. Reading the DOM, with `?`, `=` and `&` folded to spaces in what it returns, works on both. Ramp's help centre titles every page "Ramp Help Center", so its citation is checked by its `h1`. Payoneer's articles carry a site-wide `h1`, so theirs is checked by the title.
- **Machine load decides whether a page can be read at all, and load goes beside every timing.** At a load average of 77 to 91 on 2026-09-18 (another lane's deliberate CPU burners, then Swift builds) the renderer froze on several pages, and the tab group reset three times. Each was read later at a load of about 10. A freeze is not a reading, and none is recorded as one.
